## The six levels

From the revised taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001). The question in the right-hand
column is the one to ask of an item when its verbs do not settle it.

| # | Level | The student… | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remember | retrieves facts and terms | Could they answer from memory alone? |
| 2 | Understand | explains, summarizes, or puts ideas in their own words | Do they have to show they grasp the meaning? |
| 3 | Apply | carries out a procedure in a given situation | Is there a known method to follow? |
| 4 | Analyze | breaks material into parts and works out how they relate | Do they have to find the structure or the cause themselves? |
| 5 | Evaluate | judges against criteria and defends the judgment | Do they have to decide, and justify the decision? |
| 6 | Create | puts elements together into something new and coherent | Is the product theirs, with no single right answer? |

## Deciding a level by reading an item

1. Find what the student hands in or does. The level is the level of that, not of the
   reading or the setup around it.
2. If several things are asked, the level is the highest one the grade depends on. A
   rubric with a criterion for "justifies the choice" makes the item Evaluate even if
   most of the work is Apply.
3. A writing assignment is not automatically Create. A reflection that summarizes the
   week is Understand; a proposal the student designs is Create.
4. Say the reason in one line, naming the part of the item that decided it.

## Why progression is not "every step higher"

Real courses dip: students apply a technique to get the material their next analysis
needs. Requiring every item to be higher than the one before would force a course plan to
misdescribe itself. The checks therefore ask only that an outcome reaches more than one level
and does not end below where it starts.

## What the tagger cannot know

- What happened in the room. A verb is evidence of intent.
- Objectives written in images, attached files, or with verbs not in the table.
- Whether a rubric's criteria match the level its instructions claim.
